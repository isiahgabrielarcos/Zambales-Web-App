People & Traditions

The Culture of Zambales

Living Heritage

Rooted in Tradition

Zambales is home to one of the Philippines' most resilient indigenous peoples — the Aeta (Agta/Ita) — as well as a rich calendar of festivals that blend Catholic tradition with pre-colonial celebrations. From the vibrant colors of the Singkaban Festival to the quiet artistry of handwoven bamboo crafts, culture runs deep in every barangay.

Annual Calendar

Festivals & Events

January 24
Feast of Ina Poon Bato
Botolan, Zambales

One of the country's oldest Marian devotions, centered on the shrine of Ina Poon Bato (Apo Apang) in Botolan. Every January, Aeta and lowland devotees gather for a pilgrimage, novena Masses, and processions — a tradition tracing back to the early Spanish missions.

March
Suman-Ibos Festival
Castillejos, Zambales

Held during Castillejos' Farmers' Week, this festival celebrates the town's beloved suman (sticky rice cake wrapped in leaves). Expect cooking and eating contests, street dancing, agricultural fairs, and giant suman that locals prepare to share with visitors.

April
Dinamulag (Mango) Festival
Iba, Zambales (Provincial Capital)

"Dinamulag" is the Sambal word for mango. Zambales' flagship festival celebrates its world-famous sweet Carabao mangoes with street dancing, float parades, mango-themed cuisine, trade fairs, and agricultural showcases at the provincial capital.

April 25 – May 1
Paynawen Festival
Iba, Zambales (Provincial Capital)

The founding-anniversary festival of Iba, the provincial capital (founded 1611). "Paynawen" means a place of rest; the week-long celebration features civic parades, cultural presentations, street dancing, and a showcase of the capital town's heritage and products.

May 3 – 4
Domorokdok Festival
Botolan, Zambales

Botolan's vibrant founding-anniversary festival, featuring colorful street dancing in indigenous-inspired costumes, ritual performances, a beauty pageant, and displays of the town's farm produce and Aeta crafts.

November 30
Binabayani Festival
Masinloc, Zambales

A dramatic mock-battle festival held on the feast of San Andres Apostol. Performers reenact the historic struggle between the Aeta, Christianized lowlanders, and "Moro" raiders through ritual dance — one of Zambales' most distinctive cultural spectacles.

Indigenous Peoples

The Aeta People

The Aeta (also called Agta or Ita) are among the first inhabitants of the Philippine archipelago, believed to have arrived over 30,000 years ago. They are semi-nomadic forest dwellers who traditionally lived by hunting, gathering, and small-scale agriculture in the Zambales mountain range.

Known for their extraordinary knowledge of medicinal plants, forest survival skills, and traditional arts such as bamboo weaving and beadwork, the Aeta have survived colonization, the Mt. Pinatubo eruption of 1991, and countless other upheavals — adapting while preserving their identity.

Responsible cultural tourism in Aeta communities supports their livelihoods and helps preserve their traditions. Visitors can participate in guided cultural experiences in communities near San Marcelino and Botolan — learning fire-making, bamboo craft, and traditional hunting techniques.

Arts & Expression

Crafts, Language & Story

The Zambal Language

Zambal (also spelled Sambal) is the indigenous Austronesian language of the Zambal people of Zambales. Closely related to Kapampangan and Tagalog, it is spoken primarily in the central and northern municipalities. The language carries centuries of oral literature — proverbs, folk songs, and epic tales of the sea and mountains.

Traditional Crafts

Bamboo weaving is central to Aeta and Zambal craftsmanship — baskets, mats, furniture, and decorative pieces. Buri (palm leaf) weaving produces hats, bags, and mats sold in local markets. Pottery and shell crafts are also part of the coastal municipality heritage along the Zambales coastline.
