People & Traditions

The Culture of Zambales

Living Heritage

Rooted in Tradition

Zambales is home to one of the Philippines' most resilient indigenous peoples — the Aeta (Agta/Ita) — as well as a rich calendar of festivals that blend Catholic tradition with pre-colonial celebrations. From the vibrant colors of the Singkaban Festival to the quiet artistry of handwoven bamboo crafts, culture runs deep in every barangay.

Annual Calendar

Festivals & Events

August
Singkaban Festival
📍 Iba, Zambales (Provincial Capital)

Iba's premier festival celebrates the founding anniversary of the province. Streets come alive with colorful bamboo (singkaban) arch displays, street dancing, cultural shows, trade fairs, and a beauty pageant. It's the most anticipated provincial celebration of the year.

March – May
Zambales Mango Festival
📍 Various municipalities

Celebrating the province's famous Carabao mangoes, this festival features mango eating contests, cooking competitions, and parades. Local farmers showcase prize mangoes and vendors line up along the highway selling everything from fresh fruit to dried mango pasalubong.

June
Subic Bay Festival
📍 Olongapo City

A week-long maritime and cultural celebration around Subic Bay with outrigger races, music concerts, cultural shows, and the Zambales seafood market. The festival also pays tribute to the city's transformation from a US naval base to a thriving commerce and tourism zone.

October
Pista ng Dagat
📍 San Antonio

A thanksgiving festival for the sea by the fishing communities of San Antonio, gateway to Anawangin and Nagsasa Coves. Includes a fluvial parade, boat blessing ceremony, beach games, and cultural presentations honoring the livelihood that the sea provides.

January
Pintakasi Festival
📍 Subic, Zambales

Named after the Tagalog concept of community cooperation, Pintakasi honors the bayanihan spirit of the Subic municipality. Features street dancing in traditional costumes, visual arts exhibitions, and performances rooted in the area's pre-colonial Kapampangan-Zambal heritage.

June 13
Feast of San Antonio de Padua
📍 San Antonio, Zambales

The patron saint celebration of San Antonio — one of the most important festivals for the municipality. Mass, processions, street fairs, and lively community gatherings mark the day. The municipality is the main jump-off for Anawangin and Nagsasa Cove adventures.

Indigenous Peoples

The Aeta People

The Aeta (also called Agta or Ita) are among the first inhabitants of the Philippine archipelago, believed to have arrived over 30,000 years ago. They are semi-nomadic forest dwellers who traditionally lived by hunting, gathering, and small-scale agriculture in the Zambales mountain range.

Known for their extraordinary knowledge of medicinal plants, forest survival skills, and traditional arts such as bamboo weaving and beadwork, the Aeta have survived colonization, the Mt. Pinatubo eruption of 1991, and countless other upheavals — adapting while preserving their identity.

Responsible cultural tourism in Aeta communities supports their livelihoods and helps preserve their traditions. Visitors can participate in guided cultural experiences in communities near San Marcelino and Botolan — learning fire-making, bamboo craft, and traditional hunting techniques.

Arts & Expression

Crafts, Language & Story

The Zambal Language

Zambal (also spelled Sambal) is the indigenous Austronesian language of the Zambal people of Zambales. Closely related to Kapampangan and Tagalog, it is spoken primarily in the central and northern municipalities. The language carries centuries of oral literature — proverbs, folk songs, and epic tales of the sea and mountains.

Traditional Crafts

Bamboo weaving is central to Aeta and Zambal craftsmanship — baskets, mats, furniture, and decorative pieces. Buri (palm leaf) weaving produces hats, bags, and mats sold in local markets. Pottery and shell crafts are also part of the coastal municipality heritage along the Zambales coastline.
