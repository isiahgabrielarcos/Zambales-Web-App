Beaches, Coves & Reefs

Aquascapes of Zambales

The Sea

Where the Ocean Calls

The Zambales coastline stretches for over 200 kilometers along the South China Sea, dotted with secluded coves, white-sand beaches, and vibrant marine ecosystems. From the famous pine-tree coves of San Antonio to the surf breaks of San Felipe and the dive sites around Subic Bay, the water here is an endless adventure.

Must-Visit

Top 5 Water Destinations

1
Cove & Camping · San Antonio

Anawangin Cove

The most iconic destination in Zambales. A pristine, isolated cove with no road access — boat only from Pundaquit — ringed by a grove of Agoho pines growing on volcanic ash. No electricity, no WiFi, just a perfect crescent of white sand, calm turquoise water, and the sound of the sea. Best experienced overnight under the stars.

2
Cove & Camping · San Antonio

Nagsasa Cove

Nagsasa is the wilder, more secluded sister of Anawangin — a longer boat ride but absolutely worth it. Its crescent bay is wider, the water calmer, and it sees fewer tourists. A river cutting through the cove empties into the sea, creating a perfect freshwater dipping pool behind the beach.

3
Bay · Olongapo / Subic

Subic Bay

A world-class dive destination with excellent visibility and multiple dive sites including WWII shipwrecks (the USS New York, El Capitan, and others) resting on the bay floor. Also popular for wakeboarding, kayaking, sailing, and dolphin watching. The bay's former US naval base history adds a unique layer of exploration.

4
Island · San Antonio

Camara & Capones Islands

Two beautiful offshore islands near Pundaquit offering snorkelling over coral gardens, cliff jumping, and lighthouse trekking on Capones (which has a Spanish-era lighthouse at its summit). Both are reachable from the same pumpboat jump-off as Anawangin.

5
Surf Break · San Felipe

San Felipe Surf Beach

The surf capital of Zambales. San Felipe is an emerging surf destination with consistent waves from July to November during the southwest monsoon. Both beginners (mellow shore breaks) and experienced surfers (reef breaks) find what they're looking for here. Board rentals and lessons are available from local surf schools.

Also Worth Visiting

More Coastal Spots

Island · Candelaria

Potipot Island

A tiny, postcard-perfect island at the northern tip of Zambales in Candelaria. Crystal-clear water surrounds white sand. A short boat ride from the mainland with facilities on-island.

Beach · San Antonio

Crystal Beach

A private beach resort with calm waters perfect for families. Known for its clear water and the gentle slope of its sandy shore. Cottages, snorkelling gear, and kayak rentals available on-site.

Cove · San Antonio

Silanguin Cove

The third major cove in the San Antonio cluster, lesser known than Anawangin and Nagsasa but equally beautiful. Very few overnight campers make it here, making it a near-private experience.

Beach · Iba

Iba Barangay Beach

The beach accessible from the provincial capital. Long stretches of dark sand (typical of Zambales' northern coast) with calm waters, local eateries lining the shore, and beautiful sunsets facing west.

Hit the Water

Sea Activities

🏄
Surfing

San Felipe is the main surf hub. Consistent swells from July–November. Lessons from local schools starting at ₱500/hour including board and instructor.

⛵
Island Hopping

Hire a pumpboat from Pundaquit to visit Anawangin, Nagsasa, Silanguin, Capones, and Camara Islands. Full-day or overnight packages available.

🤿
Diving & Snorkelling

Subic Bay offers world-class wreck diving. Capones and Camara Islands have vibrant coral gardens for snorkellers. Multiple dive shops in the Subic area.

🏖
Beach Resorts

From the exclusive to the family-friendly, beach resorts dot the entire coastline. Crystal Beach, Liwliwa, and San Narciso offer day and overnight packages.
