Tips & Guides

Plan Smart

Everything You Need to Know

Whether it's your first trip to Zambales or your tenth, having the right information makes all the difference. Here's a practical guide covering transportation, packing, budget, safety, etiquette, and the best times to visit.

Getting There & Around
  • Victory Liner buses depart from Cubao, Pasay, and Caloocan to Olongapo and Iba daily
  • By car: NLEX → SCTEX → Olongapo/Subic; allow 3–4 hours from Metro Manila
  • Tricycles are the main local transport within towns — short rides cost ₱10–₱30
  • Jeepneys connect most municipalities along the national highway
  • Van for hire services available in Olongapo and Iba for group travel
  • No Grab/Uber outside Olongapo; negotiate tricycle and habal-habal fares upfront
What to Pack
  • Reef-safe sunscreen (SPF 50+) — the sun is intense along the coast
  • Dry bag for boat rides and pumpboat transfers to coves
  • Insect repellent — especially for camping in the coves
  • Headlamp for overnight camping (no electricity in Anawangin/Nagsasa)
  • Trekking shoes if doing the Pinatubo hike or mountain trails
  • Light rain jacket — useful during shoulder and wet months
  • Reusable water bottle and trash bag (leave no trace)
Best Time to Visit
  • November–May: Dry season — ideal for beaches, coves, and Pinatubo trek
  • March–May: Peak mango season; also peak tourist season — book ahead
  • July–September: Surf season in San Felipe; fewer crowds at beaches
  • June–October: Wet season — expect rain, but also lush mountain scenery
  • Avoid Holy Week and long weekends unless booking well in advance
  • Weekdays offer quieter coves and lower boat charter prices
Budget Guide
  • Pumpboat hire (Anawangin): ₱1,500–₱2,000 for a whole boat (10–15 pax)
  • Pinatubo 4×4 + guide: ₱1,000–₱1,800/person depending on group size
  • Camping permit (Anawangin/Nagsasa): ₱50 environmental fee per person
  • Meals at carinderias: ₱80–₱150 per full meal
  • Surf lesson (San Felipe): ₱500 including board rental for 1.5 hours
  • Budget per day: ₱800–₱1,500 (excluding travel from Manila)
Safety Tips
  • Always register with the tourism office before heading to remote coves or peaks
  • Never swim alone at night — there are no lifeguards at the coves
  • Withdraw cash before leaving town — no ATMs near coves or mountain trailheads
  • Bring at least 2 liters of water per person for day hikes; more for Pinatubo
  • Check weather forecasts before island hopping — sea conditions change quickly
  • Coast Guard and LGU tourism officers are stationed at Pundaquit jump-off point
Local Etiquette
  • Pack out all your trash — leave no trace, especially in natural coves
  • Respect the Aeta communities if you visit cultural sites — ask before photographing
  • Greet locals with "Magandang umaga/hapon/gabi" — it goes a long way
  • Tip your boatmen and guides — it's not mandatory but deeply appreciated
  • Do not collect coral, shells, or plant life from protected areas
  • Be patient with local transport schedules — things run on "island time" here
