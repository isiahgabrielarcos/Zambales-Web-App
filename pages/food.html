Flavours of the Province

The Food of Zambales

Table & Taste

Coastal Cuisine & Sweetness

Zambales cuisine is defined by the sea. Fresh-caught fish, shellfish, and the iconic kinilaw (ceviche) are staples of every local table. Inland, the volcanic soil produces world-class Carabao mangoes — a fruit so beloved it has its own festival. From roadside stalls to family-run carinderias, eating in Zambales is a genuine local experience.

What to Eat

Top Local Dishes

Kinilaw na Tanigue

Freshly caught Spanish mackerel (tanigue) cured in vinegar and calamansi with ginger, onion, and chili. The most popular appetizer and pulutan in Zambales — best eaten within hours of the catch.

Seafood · Raw
Inihaw na Bangus

Whole milkfish stuffed with tomatoes, onions, and ginger then grilled over charcoal until the skin is charred and crispy. A beloved Filipino classic done exceptionally well at seaside eateries along the Zambales coast.

Grilled · Seafood
Zambales Carabao Mango

The undisputed star of Zambales produce. The Carabao variety grown in volcanic soil produces an exceptionally sweet, almost fibre-free mango. Eaten fresh from roadside stalls, dried as pasalubong, or transformed into mango float, jam, and local sweets.

Fruit · Seasonal (Mar–Jun)
Sinuglaw

A delicious hybrid dish combining sinugba (grilled pork belly) with kinilaw (vinegar-cured fish). The smoky pork and the tangy, citrusy fish create a remarkable contrast. A quintessential Zambales pulutan served with cold drinks at local bars.

Pulutan · Fusion
Sugpo sa Gata

Giant tiger prawns cooked in rich coconut milk with ginger, garlic, and siling labuyo. The prawns come fresh from local waters, making this dish remarkably sweet and flavourful at its best along the Zambales coast.

Seafood · Coconut
Bringhe

A sticky rice dish cooked in coconut milk and wrapped in banana leaves — the Kapampangan and Zambal counterpart to paella. Traditionally cooked for fiestas and special occasions, it's a deeply flavourful festive rice that tells of the region's culinary heritage.

Rice Dish · Festive
Zambales Mango Float

Made with locally grown Carabao mangoes, this no-bake icebox cake of cream, graham crackers, and thick mango slices is a staple dessert in Zambales kitchens and sold by local vendors throughout the province.

Dessert · Local Favourite
Sinigang na Isda

Sour tamarind soup with fresh locally caught fish. In Zambales the souring agent is often sampalok (tamarind) from backyard trees, and the fish comes directly from the morning's catch — making it fresher and more intensely flavoured than in the city.

Soup · Seafood

Where to Eat

Top Food Areas & Markets

Olongapo Night Market
📍 Olongapo City

The largest food market strip in Zambales opens in the evenings along the Olongapo bay area. Dozens of stalls serve everything from grilled seafood and isaw to Korean-influenced street food — a legacy of the city's cosmopolitan history near the former US naval base.

Iba Public Market Carinderia Row
📍 Iba (Provincial Capital)

The carinderia stalls inside and surrounding Iba's public market serve the most authentic and affordable provincial cooking — from kare-kare and kaldereta to grilled tilapia and pinakbet. The market also has the freshest local produce including seasonal mangoes and vegetables from upland farms.

Pundaquit Bayside Eateries
📍 Pundaquit, San Antonio

The jump-off point for island hopping is lined with rustic seafood restaurants. Boats unload their catch here and the eateries cook it same-day. Grilled talakitok, sinigang na labahita, and kinilaw na tanigue are the go-to orders before or after a cove excursion.

Zambales Pride

The Mango Capital of the Philippines

Zambales is home to the Philippines' most celebrated variety of mango — the Carabao. Grown in soil enriched by centuries of volcanic activity, the mangoes here have an unrivalled sweetness, minimal fibre, and a rich golden colour that signals peak ripeness.

Best season: March to June. Buy them fresh from roadside vendors along the national highway. Prices drop dramatically in-season — expect to pay a fraction of what you'd pay in Manila for superior quality.
