What To Do

Unforgettable Experiences

Every type of adventure is waiting for you along Zambales' shores and highlands.

🤿

Diving & Snorkelling

Explore vibrant coral gardens and WWII shipwrecks in the warm waters of Subic Bay and beyond.

🥾

Volcano Trekking

Hike through lahar fields and lush forest to reach the emerald crater lake of Mt. Pinatubo.

⛵

Island Hopping

Charter a pumpboat and drift between secluded coves — Anawangin, Nagsasa, Camara Island.

🏄

Surfing & Watersports

Ride the waves at San Narciso or Iba, or try kayaking, paddleboarding, and kitesurfing.

🎋

Aeta Cultural Tours

Visit indigenous Aeta communities, learn traditional fire-making, and taste authentic forest cuisine.

🍤

Seafood & Local Cuisine

Feast on grilled bangus, fresh kinilaw, and the famed Zambales mangoes — sweetest in the Philippines.

📸

Sunset Photography

Iba and Palauig face the West Philippine Sea — golden-hour skies here are simply extraordinary.

🌿

Eco-Camping

Sleep under the stars at Anawangin or Nagsasa cove, lulled by waves with no electricity — pure bliss.

About the Province

Land of Resilience
& Natural Bounty

Zambales is a first-class province on the western coast of Luzon, bordered by the Zambales Mountains to the east and the shimmering West Philippine Sea to the west. Its history is as layered as its landscape — from pre-colonial Aeta communities to Spanish galleon routes to the eruption of Mt. Pinatubo in 1991, which reshaped the very face of the province.

Today Zambales stands as a testament to renewal. Former lahar plains bloom with mangoes, the coastal towns thrive with fishing and tourism, and the indigenous heritage of the Aeta people is celebrated and preserved alongside modern progress.

Before You Go

Essential Travel Tips

01

Best Time to Visit

November to May is the dry season — ideal for beach trips, island hopping, and the Pinatubo trek. June to October brings rain, but also lush greenery and fewer crowds.

02

Getting There

From Manila, take the NLEX/SCTEX to Olongapo (around 3 hours by car). Victory Liner and other bus companies service the route from Cubao and Pasay daily.

03

Island Hopping Boats

Charter boats at Pundaquit Beach in San Antonio for Anawangin and Nagsasa. Book early during Holy Week and long weekends — these coves fill up fast.

04

Leave No Trace

Zambales' ecosystems are fragile and precious. Pack out your trash, avoid touching corals, and respect the indigenous communities you visit.

05

Local Currency

Bring sufficient cash — ATMs are sparse outside Olongapo and Iba. Most beachside cottages, boat operators, and small eateries don't accept cards.

06

Try the Mangoes!

Zambales mangoes are legendary in the Philippines. Pick up fresh or dried mangoes from roadside stalls along the national highway — a delicious take-home treat.
